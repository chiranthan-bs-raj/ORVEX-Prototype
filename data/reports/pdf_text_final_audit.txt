ORVEX
Origin and Relationship Verification Explorer
Project tagline: Trace the Origin. Reveal the Connections.
Project title: AI System for Detecting Deepfake Campaigns and Coordinated Manipulation
Project category: Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, Digital Forensics, Media Integrity, and 
Trust and Safety
1. Problem Statement
Today, AI can create fake videos, fake audio, images, and other types of misleading content. However, 
the problem is not limited to creating one fake video. The same video can be edited in many ways and 
spread across different social media platforms.
For example, someone creates or obtains a video and then makes several versions of it:
 One person trims the video.
 Another person changes its audio.
 Another person adds misleading captions.
 Another person combines the video with AI-generated audio.
 Another person creates new versions from an already manipulated video.
 These versions are shared through different accounts and social media platforms.
All these videos may look different, even though they are connected to the same original video or to 
one another.
Traditional deepfake detection mainly focuses on deciding whether an individual piece of media is real 
or AI-generated. Such detection does not necessarily reveal the complete relationship between different 
versions or explain how they are connected.
Forensic investigators may need to find these videos one by one, compare them manually, and 
determine where they came from. This takes time and makes it difficult to understand how the content 
has spread.
The main problem is the lack of an effective, unified system that can identify relationships 
between original and manipulated media, trace their possible origins, and help investigators 
discover connected manipulation campaigns across supported online platforms.
ORVEX aims to solve this problem by using AI and machine learning to discover media relationships, 
build connection graphs, identify possible manipulation methods, and support the investigation of 
coordinated campaigns.
2. What We Are Building
We are building ORVEX — Origin and Relationship Verification Explorer, an AI-powered platform 
that helps users investigate related videos and other media.
Instead of only predicting whether a video is real or fake, ORVEX will focus on finding connections 
between media items.
2
For example, when a user uploads one reference video, the system should be able to:
1. Analyze the uploaded video.
2. Search supported data sources for related content.
3. Find videos that may be derived from the reference video.
4. Identify possible changes such as trimming, audio replacement, or caption addition.
5. Discover connections between the reference video and other versions.
6. Build a graph showing the relationships.
7. Show where the content was found and when it was observed, when this information is available.
8. Help investigators understand how the related content may have spread.
The project will be developed in two main phases.
Phase 1 — Relationship Learning: Create a controlled dataset from real source videos, generate 
manipulated versions, record their relationships, and train a machine learning model to identify 
connections.
Phase 2 — Relationship Discovery: Accept a reference video, search supported platforms or datasets, 
find related media, and build a graph showing the likely connections.
Both phases will contribute to one final product: ORVEX.
3. Target Users
3.1 Digital Forensic Investigators
They can use ORVEX to compare media, trace possible origins, organize evidence, and reduce the time 
spent investigating individual videos.
3.2 Social Media Trust and Safety Teams
They can use the system to investigate related posts, identify suspicious distribution patterns, and 
prioritize content for human review.
3.3 Journalists and Fact-Checkers
They can use ORVEX to compare different versions of a video and investigate changes that may alter its 
meaning.
3.4 Researchers
AI and machine learning researchers can use the system to study media relationships, deepfake 
detection, manipulation methods, and campaign detection.
3.5 Cybercrime Investigation Teams
Where legally authorized, investigators can use the system to organize digital evidence and examine 
connections between media, posts, accounts, and timelines.
ORVEX will support investigations. It will not automatically declare a person guilty or label an account 
malicious simply because it shares related content.

4. Existing Problems and Gaps
The project addresses the following problems.
4.1 Individual Video Detection
A conventional detector may classify a video as real or fake, but that result alone does not explain 
where the video came from or which other videos are connected to it.
4.2 Manual Investigation
Investigators may need to inspect many videos separately. This makes it difficult to identify all the 
related versions efficiently.
4.3 Manipulated Versions Look Different
Trimming, cropping, compression, audio replacement, captions, and other changes can make related 
videos look different.
4.4 Missing Relationship Information
A dataset may contain real and fake labels without recording the relationships between original media 
and manipulated versions.
4.5 Platform Limitations
Different social media platforms have different APIs, permissions, and search capabilities. Finding all 
versions of a video across the internet is not always possible.
4.6 Missing Campaign-Level Analysis
Finding related media does not automatically reveal whether multiple accounts are coordinating their 
activities. Media relationships and account coordination must be analyzed separately.
4.7 New Manipulation Methods
A model trained on known transformations may fail when it encounters unfamiliar editing methods or 
newly developed AI generation techniques.
ORVEX will address these gaps through relationship learning, provenance tracking, graph analysis, 
cross-modal analysis, and robustness testing.
5. Proposed Solution
The proposed solution is divided into two phases.
Phase 1: Dataset Creation and Model Training
The first phase will create a new dataset containing original videos and manipulated versions with 
known relationships.
The starting point will be real source videos. These videos will be used to generate controlled variants.
For example, one source video may produce:
4
 Variant A: Trimmed video.
 Variant B: Video with misleading captions.
 Variant C: Video with replaced audio.
 Variant D: Recompressed video.
 Variant E: A new edit derived from Variant A.
Every video will have a unique ID. Each manipulated version will have a parent ID, transformation 
label, and relationship record.
The model will learn to recognize these relationships.
Phase 2: Reference Video Investigation
After the model has been trained and evaluated, the user will upload a reference video.
ORVEX will search the available sources, collect candidate videos, compare them, identify likely 
relationships, and create a graph.
For example, if the system finds ten related videos, it should explain which ones are likely direct 
derivatives of the reference, which ones may have come from other variants, and which relationships 
remain uncertain.
The system will also connect available post information, account identifiers, platforms, and timestamps 
to help investigate possible campaigns.
Important: Finding similar videos is not the same as proving that one was created from another. The 
system will use confidence scores and supporting evidence, and uncertain relationships will be marked 
for review.
6. Features and Modules
Module 1: Dataset Creation
 Import source videos.
 Identify duplicate files.
 Assign unique IDs to original videos.
 Generate controlled manipulated variants.
 Record transformation types and parent IDs.
 Save the known relationships between media items.
Module 2: Media Processing
 Extract video frames.
 Extract audio.
 Extract available text and captions.
 Use OCR to read text inside images and video frames.
 Generate visual, audio, and text representations.
 Store useful fingerprints and extracted features.
Module 3: Relationship Detection
 Compare two videos or media items.
5
 Compare a group of five or six videos.
 Estimate whether two items share a source.
 Identify possible direct and indirect derivation.
 Separate likely matches from uncertain matches.
 Return confidence scores and supporting evidence.
Module 4: Manipulation Analysis
 Identify possible trimming and cropping.
 Detect changes in captions and visible text.
 Compare audio tracks.
 Detect possible audio replacement.
 Examine visual and audio inconsistencies.
 Support separate AI-generated media detection models where suitable training data is available.
Module 5: Relationship Graph
 Represent each media item as a node.
 Represent relationships as edges.
 Show the original video and its variants.
 Show likely parent-child relationships.
 Display the possible manipulation type for each connection.
 Support graph search and filtering.
Module 6: Platform Integration
 Connect to one or two selected platforms first.
 Use permitted APIs or authorized data sources.
 Search for accessible candidate posts.
 Retrieve available post IDs, URLs, timestamps, and metadata.
 Normalize the results into a common format.
 Handle rate limits and unavailable content.
Module 7: Campaign Detection
 Group related posts and media into candidate clusters.
 Analyze posting times and available account information.
 Examine cross-posting and repeated distribution patterns.
 Identify possible coordination signals.
 Keep media similarity separate from evidence of coordinated behavior.
Module 8: Investigation Dashboard
 Upload a reference video.
 Start an investigation.
 View related media.
 Compare the reference with discovered variants.
 Explore the relationship graph.
 View manipulation evidence and confidence scores.
6
 Inspect source links and timestamps when available.
 Export an investigation report.
Module 9: Model Evaluation
 Measure relationship detection precision and recall.
 Measure false positives.
 Evaluate graph and cluster quality.
 Test unseen manipulation methods.
 Measure time-to-detection.
 Record model versions and experiment results.
7. End-to-End Workflow
Phase 1: Training Workflow
Step 1 — Collect original videos
Collect a suitable set of real source videos from authorized datasets or other permitted sources.
Step 2 — Check duplicates
Identify repeated files and determine which videos represent unique source content.
Step 3 — Generate manipulated variants
Create controlled versions through trimming, cropping, caption addition, audio replacement, 
recompression, and other selected transformations.
Step 4 — Record relationships
Store the source ID, parent ID, transformation type, and relationship between every original and 
manipulated version.
Step 5 — Extract features
Process the media to obtain useful visual, audio, and textual information.
Step 6 — Train the model
Train a relationship model to distinguish related media from unrelated media and, where the labels 
support it, predict relationship types.
Step 7 — Test the model
Evaluate it using source videos and transformations that were not used during training.
Step 8 — Build the graph
Use the predicted relationships to construct a graph and compare it against the known ground-truth 
graph.
Phase 2: Investigation Workflow
Step 1 — Upload a reference video
The user selects a video that needs investigation.
Step 2 — Analyze the reference
7
ORVEX extracts the required features and creates a searchable representation.
Step 3 — Search for related media
The system searches supported platforms, authorized sources, and available datasets.
Step 4 — Collect candidate videos
The system gathers accessible candidate videos and their available metadata.
Step 5 — Compare the videos
The trained model compares the reference with the candidates and also compares candidate items with 
one another.
Step 6 — Predict relationships
The system estimates direct relationships, shared-source relationships, and possible chains of 
derivation.
Step 7 — Analyze possible manipulation
The system identifies evidence of changes such as trimming, caption addition, or audio replacement 
where its methods support those conclusions.
Step 8 — Build the graph
ORVEX connects media items and adds platform posts, accounts, and timelines where evidence is 
available.
Step 9 — Identify candidate campaigns
The system groups related items and examines available distribution patterns to find possible 
coordination.
Step 10 — Display results
The dashboard presents the graph, candidate relationships, supporting evidence, confidence scores, 
source information, and uncertain findings.
8. Technology Stack
The following is the recommended technology stack for ORVEX. Each tool has a defined role, so the 
project does not become unnecessarily complicated.
8.1 Frontend
 React with TypeScript: Build the web application.
 Vite: Run the development server and build the frontend.
 Tailwind CSS: Design the interface.
 React Flow: Display the media relationship graph.
 Recharts: Display model evaluation metrics and investigation statistics.
8.2 Backend
 Python: Main programming language.
 FastAPI: Create backend APIs.
 Pydantic: Validate input and output data.
8
 FFmpeg: Process video and audio files.
 OpenCV: Extract and analyze video frames.
 Background job processing: Handle lengthy media processing and investigations.
8.3 AI and Machine Learning
 PyTorch: Train and run machine learning models.
 scikit-learn: Build baseline models and evaluate performance.
 Perceptual hashing: Find exact or near-duplicate visual content.
 Visual embeddings: Compare frames and video content.
 Audio embeddings: Compare audio content.
 Speech transcription: Convert accessible speech into text.
 OCR: Extract text from video frames.
 Text embeddings: Compare captions and textual content.
 Contrastive learning or metric learning: Learn relationships between related and unrelated 
media.
The initial model should focus on media relationships. Separate models can be added for AI-generated 
media detection and manipulation classification.
8.4 Applied AI and Agent Framework
Python
Python will be the main language for AI, machine learning, media processing, and backend 
development.
LangChain
LangChain will be the primary framework for integrating language models with tools and structured 
workflows.
LangGraph
LangGraph will manage multi-step AI workflows, including investigation planning, candidate analysis, 
evidence gathering, and review stages.
Agents and Multi-Agent Architecture
The system may use specialized agents for different tasks:
9. Investigation Agent: Manages the investigation workflow.
10. Media Analysis Agent: Coordinates media-processing tools and receives their results.
11. Relationship Analysis Agent: Organizes relationship predictions from the trained ML models.
12. Platform Search Agent: Calls the supported platform connectors.
13. Campaign Analysis Agent: Examines the relationship graph and available distribution 
information.
14. Report Agent: Produces a readable investigation report from the collected evidence.
These agents should use tools and model outputs rather than inventing evidence. Core media matching 
and relationship scoring should remain in tested, reproducible ML components.
9
Tool Use and Function Calling
Tool use and function calling will let an AI agent request specific actions, such as:
 Process a video.
 Search a supported platform.
 Compare two media items.
 Retrieve a relationship score.
 Query the graph.
 Generate an investigation report.
Each tool will have a defined input and output format.
Model Context Protocol (MCP)
MCP may be used to provide a standard interface between agents and approved external tools or data 
sources. It is optional for the first prototype and should be introduced only where it simplifies 
integration.
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
RAG can retrieve relevant investigation records, media metadata, transformation logs, and supporting 
evidence before the report is generated.
RAG will not replace the relationship detection model. It will help the language model retrieve and 
explain recorded information.
n8n and Webhooks
n8n can be self-hosted to coordinate event-based workflows.
For example:
 A new investigation is created.
 A webhook starts a backend job.
 The backend processes the media.
 The result is stored.
 A webhook or workflow updates the investigation status.
 The report becomes available to the dashboard.
The core ML pipeline should remain independent of n8n so it can continue to operate if the workflow 
automation service is unavailable.
Recommended AI Architecture
FastAPI will receive requests. LangGraph will coordinate the investigation workflow. LangChain will 
connect the language model with the required tools. The trained PyTorch models will perform the 
actual media relationship predictions. The database and graph layer will store the evidence and results.
8.5 Database and Storage
 SQLite: Suitable for the first local prototype.
 PostgreSQL: An option for a larger multi-user system.
 Neo4j: A recommended graph database option if graph queries become complex.
 Local file storage: Store authorized source media and generated variants.
10
 Vector index: Optional storage for media embeddings and similarity search.
SQLite and Neo4j do not have to be introduced together from the beginning. The first prototype can use 
SQLite and graph-oriented relationship tables, with Neo4j added if the project benefits from a dedicated 
graph database.
8.6 External APIs
The first implementation should connect to one or two platforms selected after checking their current 
API permissions and available search features.
Possible candidates include Reddit, Telegram, or Instagram, subject to their respective access rules.
The integration layer should support:
 Search requests.
 Available post metadata.
 Source URLs and post IDs.
 Timestamps and account identifiers where permitted.
 Rate-limit handling.
 Error handling and retries.
 Source tracking and access monitoring.
The system must not assume that every platform allows unrestricted video search or downloading.
8.7 Testing and Deployment
 Docker: Run services in a repeatable environment.
 Pytest: Test backend APIs, data processing, and ML functions.
 Vitest: Test frontend components.
 Playwright: Test complete user workflows.
 Experiment tracking: Record datasets, model versions, and evaluation results.
 Structured logging: Record processing events and errors.
8.8 Recommended Overall Architecture
React and TypeScript will provide the interface. FastAPI will manage requests and investigations. 
LangGraph and LangChain will coordinate agent workflows. Python-based ML components will process 
media and predict relationships. SQLite or PostgreSQL will store investigation records. Graph storage 
will represent relationships. Authorized platform connectors will discover accessible candidates. n8n 
webhooks may coordinate background events.
9. Novelty and Differentiation
The main difference between ORVEX and a basic deepfake detector is its focus on relationships.
9.1 From Individual Detection to Relationship Detection
Instead of only predicting whether a video is real or fake, ORVEX will investigate how multiple media 
items may be connected.
11
9.2 A Custom Relationship Dataset
The project will create a dataset containing original videos, manipulated variants, transformation 
labels, parent IDs, and known relationships.
This will allow the model to learn from relationship labels instead of relying only on real/fake labels.
9.3 Graph-Based Investigation
The system will show media relationships as a graph so investigators can follow likely connections 
between the original and its variants.
9.4 Cross-Modal Analysis
The system will combine visual, audio, and text evidence to identify relationships that may not be 
obvious from one type of data alone.
9.5 Campaign-Level Analysis
ORVEX will combine media provenance with post and timeline information to investigate possible 
coordinated activity.
9.6 Robustness Testing
The model will be evaluated on transformations and source videos that were not used during training.
The project's novelty must be demonstrated through a literature review and experiments against 
relevant existing methods. These features are the proposed contributions, not proof that no other 
system has implemented them.
